Yilin Wu

Research Assistant at Ji Lab · PhD Applicant

I'm Yilin Wu (吴依霖; publications as Yilin Elaine Wu), a Research Assistant in Ji Lab at Chinese Institute for Brain Research. I recently graduated in psychology from Nanjing University and am currently preparing PhD applications in cognitive science and related fields.

My current work examines how people discover and use structure in complex environments to support efficient learning, combining behavioral experiments with computational modeling. I work under the supervision of Prof. Ni Ji and welcome conversations about related research and collaboration.

Research Interests

My research centers on how humans learn, reason, and build structured representations that support adaptive behavior.

  • Structured exploration, learning, and representation building in complex environments.
  • Reasoning, abstraction, and decision-making.
  • Computational cognitive models of learning and behavior.
  • Developmental cognitive science and adaptive behavior.

Short Talk

Structured Exploration from Latent Task Dimensions Supports Efficient Human Learning.

Wu, Y.E.

Beijing Brain Conference 2026 · Thematic forum: “Neural Mechanisms of Psychiatric Disorders”

  • Discussed how people use latent task dimensions to organize exploration in complex environments, supporting more efficient learning.
  • Highlighted emerging evidence that structured exploration differs across levels of trait anxiety, suggesting a link between exploratory strategies and affective vulnerability.
